# Singapore Penal Code section 84 pilot: decision report

**Status:** provisional research decision for maintainer and Singapore-qualified criminal-law review, 13 September 2026. This is not legal advice, a claim of current-law completeness, or an executable rule package. Evidence is itemised in the [source ledger](SINGAPORE-SECTION-84-SOURCE-LEDGER.md); proposed propositions and uncertainties are in the [doctrinal matrix](SINGAPORE-SECTION-84-DOCTRINAL-MATRIX.md); the [review packet](SINGAPORE-SECTION-84-LEGAL-REVIEW-PACKET.md) is the next handoff.

## Executive recommendation

**Narrow section 84 to a source-pinned statutory-structure and representability pilot; do not yet run it as a reviewed Singapore legal assessment.** Confidence is **moderate** in that architecture decision, **low** in the completeness of directly verified current statutory text because SSO and Gazette endpoints returned HTTP 403. The post-1-March-2022 s 84(1)(b)/(2) relationship is material, and the Court of Appeal quoted that structure in *Roszaidi* [2022] SGCA 75 [87], while saying s 84 was not the appeal issue [88] [S02, S09]. The 2022 and 2025 CPC changes make a combined s 84/disposition pilot especially unsafe [S12–S15].

The concrete next milestone is **a qualified legal review of a pinned, exact-source-byte section-84 structure and applicability checklist**, using a `ModelBundle-v1` candidate whose positive scope is explicitly limited to s 84 and its cross-instrument review context. It should produce no court outcome and should not turn the corpus s 84 file into a legal oracle. A later executable offence-plus-defence pilot can be considered after the gates below. Among possible anchors, **Penal Code s 323 (voluntarily causing hurt) is the leading conditional candidate**, but its definitions and statutory exclusions make it unsuitable for immediate implementation without additional review. No anchor is approved by this report.

## What the repository can and cannot presently say

The seven closed [Haskell fragments](../../rewrite/haskell/README.md) evaluate a selected root rule over exact leaf IDs, supplied proof classifications and acyclic technical presumptive routes; they return ordered branch, exception, dependency, penalty and term traces. [AcyclicGuardedExceptions-v1](ACYCLIC-GUARDED-EXCEPTIONS-V1.md) can technically defeat an eligible branch when a registered target rule is satisfied. [SuppliedProofStatus-v1](SUPPLIED-PROOF-STATUS-V1.md) requires an external `proved | not_proved | unresolved` classification and does **not** decide evidence sufficiency; [RegisteredPresumptionDerivations-v1](REGISTERED-PRESUMPTION-DERIVATIONS-V1.md) derives only registered technical support, not Evidence Act doctrine. [TypedBooleanFacts-v1](TYPED-BOOLEAN-FACTS-V1.md) checks burden/standard annotations for exact agreement; that check is not proof of discharge. [GuardedPenaltySelection-v1](GUARDED-PENALTY-SELECTION-V1.md) selects candidates only from final-satisfied root branches; [PenaltyTerms-v1](PENALTY-TERMS-V1.md) describes terms, not a sentence. [ClosedBooleanBranches-v1](CLOSED-BOOLEAN-BRANCHES-V1.md) provides source-ordered All/Any/alternative structure.

[ModelBundle-v1](MODEL-BUNDLE-V1.md) can freeze a source artifact and map exact semantic IDs to source-text bytes, state positive scope and exclusions, and receive unsigned review assertions. It does not authenticate a statute, establish point-in-time applicability or certify legal correctness. [ModelBundleChangeSet-v1](MODEL-BUNDLE-CHANGE-IMPACT-PROVISIONAL-SPEC.md) reports mechanical core differences; it does not decide amendment or transfer review. The accepted [criminal-outcome boundary](CRIMINAL-OUTCOME-BOUNDARY-DECISION-REPORT.md) identifies no charge, accused, trial, verdict or order in KernelInput/Result. Technical branch `satisfied` cannot be relabelled conviction; `not_satisfied` cannot be relabelled acquittal.

The checked-in [s 84 `.yh` corpus entry](../../library/penal_code/s84_act_person_unsound_mind/statute.yh) carries several `effective` dates and a source-like text, but no point-in-time authenticated source or qualified legal approval. [Runtime subsection tests](../../tests/test_runtime_subsections.py) and [Canonical IR tests](../../tests/test_canonical_ir.py) assert structural behavior/citations, not statute fidelity. Legacy [Python outcome resolution](../../src/yuho/eval/statute_evaluator.py) can map rule satisfaction to court-like labels and substitute penalty targets; the [rewrite audit](REPOSITORY-AUDIT.md) and [migration contract](MIGRATION-CONTRACT.md) reject that as a legal oracle. Neither these tests nor source hashes make the corpus current law.

The future **substantive** target is a frozen post-1-March-2022 s 84 expression at a reviewer-selected *alleged-conduct date*, together with recorded amendment/commencement evidence. The **procedural** context is a separately pinned CPC expression at a *proceeding date*; one date must not stand in for both. Retrieval date, revised-edition date and bundle-validation date establish neither legal applicability nor completeness. Later amendments and savings require independent checks. If an alleged act predates 1 March 2022, the s 84(2) conjunction must not be applied by default.

## Proposed positive scope and exclusions

The initial positive scope is an inspectable **structure map**, not executable Singapore doctrine: s 84(1)'s common temporal/causal premise; alternative nature-of-act, wrongfulness and complete-control-incapacity limbs; s 84(2)'s paired wrongfulness tests; source/version pointers; and explicit dependency questions for EA s 107 and CPC Part 13 Division 5. Label each relation as statute text, judicial interpretation, or provisional encoding convention. `ModelBundle-v1` positive semantic IDs should cover only items actually mapped to frozen source-text spans. A source note about CPC is **not** a claim to model CPC procedure.

Exclude evidence assessment, psychiatric diagnosis, witness credibility, fact finding, burden discharge, irrebuttable or doctrinal presumptions, accused identity, charge/plea, fitness to plead, judicial s 84 determination, CPC act finding, custody/supervision/release, penalty imposition, sentence, appeal and legal advice. Exclude intoxication (PC ss 85–86), diminished responsibility (s 300 Exception 7), automatism, other general exceptions, private defence, cross-Act applicability, and any broad claim to a complete Penal Code defence engine. Do not use a synthetic fact assignment as a Singapore legal conclusion.

## Anchor-offence comparison

| Candidate | Dependencies and appeal evidence | Why not yet approved |

| PC s 323, voluntarily causing hurt | Requires at least hurt s 319, voluntariness s 321, punishment s 323, plus explicit s 323A/s 334 exclusions in indexed s 323 [S04]. | PC s 279, rash driving/riding on a public way | Compact offence/punishment text in SSO index, but rashness/negligence, endangerment and public-way scope carry interpretive and potential road-traffic dependencies [S04]. | Less clear synthetic mental-state classification; may distract from s 84’s issue. |

Do not choose homicide as a first anchor simply because reported s 84 cases involve homicide. Section 323 is only a **review candidate**. A defence-only structure map should precede an executable s 323/s 84 composition, which must have a pinned conduct date, complete positive scope and explicit omitted alternatives. A technically defeated s 323 branch would remain a model result, not an acquittal.

## Risk, gates and reversal conditions

The decisive risks are an unverified statutory expression; silent import of pre-2022 wrongfulness or pre-2019 volition holdings; treating a medical label as legal incapacity; applying the EA absence presumption as a generic technical default; mixing act-time responsibility with trial-time fitness; and presenting a selected penalty or exception-defeated branch as a court disposition. [S09] contrasts s 84 with diminished responsibility while expressly not deciding s 84; [S16] decides an old-CPC act finding on accepted s 84 facts; neither supplies a complete post-2025 procedure.

Before any reviewed jurisdiction-specific executable example: (1) acquire official exact PC/EA/CPC and commencement bytes, record hashes, versions and independently checked spans; (2) fix alleged-conduct and proceeding dates and any saving/transitional issues; (3) have a Singapore-qualified criminal-law reviewer approve the s 84 propositions, burden/standard and continuing prosecution duties, pre/post-amendment case treatment, and whether an anchor plus other exceptions is complete; (4) pin exact positive semantic IDs and exclusions in a `ModelBundle-v1` candidate; (5) obtain a new unsigned, digest-specific asserted review record and state that identity/competence remain unauthenticated; (6) separately decide any missing temporal-causal/exception legal semantics and the permissible product language. No reviewed fixture should arise from Python parity, the corpus, or a hash alone.

Reverse the narrowed recommendation toward an executable anchor only if these gates pass and the reviewer determines a bounded offence-plus-s 84 model gives a defensible, expressly technical question. Reverse toward **deferral** if official point-in-time sources remain unavailable, the correct temporal law cannot be selected, or the reviewer finds that an externally assigned temporal-causal composite leaf would conceal the decisive legal question. Do not begin a new protocol fragment or executable pilot from this report alone.
