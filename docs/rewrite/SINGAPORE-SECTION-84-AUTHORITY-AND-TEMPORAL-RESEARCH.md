## Executive conclusion

**Proceed only to a qualified review of the source-pinned, non-executable section 84 structure. Do not propose an executable Singapore `ModelBundle-v1` yet.** The actual Gazette PDFs establish that the Criminal Law Reform Act 2019, s 27 replaced Penal Code s 84 with effect from **1 January 2020**, and that Criminal Law (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 2021, s 8 amended its wrongfulness route with effect from **1 March 2022**. The latter date is directly stated in S 146/2022, cl 2, which commenced all of Act 23/2021 except s 3. [Act 15/2019, s 27](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2019/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf); [S 849/2019, cl 2](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2019/Legislative%20Supplements/Subsidiary%20Legislation%20Supplement/849.pdf); [Act 23/2021, s 8](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2021/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf); [S 146/2022, cl 2](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2022/Legislative%20Supplements/Subsidiary%20Legislation%20Supplement/146.pdf).

The inserted s 84(2) **textually** joins incapacity to know ordinary-standards wrongfulness **and** incapacity to know wrongfulness contrary to law for the s 84(1)(b) route. The [minister's second-reading speech, paras 48–50](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/second-reading-of-criminal-law-miscellaneous-amendments-bill-speech-by-mr-desmond-tan/) calls this conjunctive and a clarification of the 2019 position, rather than a substantive change. That is official legislative context, not a judicial interpretation of a contested post-2022 s 84 case. The 2019 text used a potentially misleading parenthetical “or” but also an illustration pointing the other way. The construction for pre-2022 conduct and any savings question therefore **remain qualified-review dependent**. [Act 15/2019, s 27 and Illustration](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2019/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf); [Act 23/2021, s 8](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2021/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf).

The Evidence Act s 107 browser capture says an accused bears the burden of proving circumstances within a Penal Code general exception and that the court presumes their absence. The directly read Court of Appeal judgment *Roshdi* [2021] SGCA 103 [72]–[80] describes this as a **legal/persuasive burden on the accused on the balance of probabilities**, while the prosecution retains the legal burden to prove the charge beyond reasonable doubt. *Jumaat* [2025] SGCA 40 [49]–[50] repeats that explanation, but concerns MDA presumptions. The **current, point-in-time EA s 107 text was not fetched directly**; SSO returned 403. Neither case adjudicated a section 84 defence. [Roshdi judgment](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2021_SGCA_103/pdf); [Jumaat judgment](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2025_SGCA_40/pdf).

## Section 84 structure: text, inference and cases

[Act 15/2019 s 27](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2019/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf) directly states a shared premise: at the time of the act, **by reason of unsoundness of mind**, the person is incapable of knowing the nature of the act, incapable of knowing relevant wrongfulness, **or** completely deprived of power to control actions. [Act 23/2021 s 8](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2021/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf) directly states that the wrongfulness route in s 84(1)(b) applies only if both s 84(2)(a) and (b) incapacities hold. That **statutory syntax** supports a provisional `All` within that one route, with `Any` across the three routes. It does **not** establish how a court evaluates disputed mental condition, causal connection, knowledge, or evidence. Nor may a plain `unsoundness AND incapacity` encode “by reason of” as though correlation proved causation.

The post-2022 conjunction is stronger than a free-floating textual guess because the Gazette uses “and” and the [minister's official explanation, paras 48–50](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/second-reading-of-criminal-law-miscellaneous-amendments-bill-speech-by-mr-desmond-tan/) expressly calls the two limbs conjunctive. It is **not** a located judicial merits holding applying s 84(2) to a contested case. *Roszaidi* [2022] SGCA 75 [87] quotes the amended provision, but [88] expressly says s 84 was **not** in issue; the appeal concerned MDA s 33B diminished responsibility for 2015 conduct. Its quote also does not prove amended s 84 governed the 2015 act. [Roszaidi judgment](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2022_SGCA_75/pdf).

**Pre-2022 treatment remains the key interpretive question.** Act 15/2019 s 27(b) used “whether ... or ...”, while its illustration denied the exception where the actor knew illegality despite believing moral rightness. The minister said in 2021 that the amendment made the already-conjunctive 2019 position clearer. That supports, but does not judicially settle, the relation between the parenthetical, illustration and later wording for pre-commencement conduct. Pre-2019 statements cannot silently settle the 2019 or 2022 expressions. [Act 15/2019, s 27](https://assets.egazette.gov.sg/2019/Legislative%20Supplements/Acts%20Supplement/23.pdf); [minister's speech, para 50](https://www.mha.gov.sg/media-room/newsroom/second-reading-of-criminal-law-miscellaneous-amendments-bill-speech-by-mr-desmond-tan/).

## Section 107, burdens and unresolved evidence

The locked EA snapshot's s 107 and Illustration (a) explicitly put proof of general-exception circumstances on the accused and instruct a **court** to presume absence. The current point-in-time EA text is still snapshot-dependent because direct SSO returned 403. It is unsafe to recode “presume absence” as a default `not_proved` fact or as a `RegisteredPresumptionDerivations-v1` route; that technical algebra has different trigger/rebuttal and burden semantics. [EA snapshot receipt](SINGAPORE-SECTION-84-SOURCE-INTAKE-REPORT.md); [Roshdi, [73]](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2021_SGCA_103/pdf).

The Court of Appeal in *Roshdi* [72]–[75] distinguishes a **legal/persuasive burden** (proving an issue to its standard) from an **evidential burden** (adducing enough evidence to put it in issue). At [73], it expressly uses EA s 107 as an example: an accused proving general-exception facts bears a legal burden on the **balance of probabilities**. At [79]–[82], the evidential threshold differs according to who bears the legal burden. The prosecution's enduring burden to prove the charge **beyond reasonable doubt** is explained in *GCK* [126]–[149] and repeated in *Roshdi* [72]. *Jumaat* [49]–[51] distinguishes a discrete accused burden from shifting the prosecution's overall burden and quotes *Roshdi* [72]–[73]; its holding concerns MDA presumptions, not s 84. [Roshdi](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2021_SGCA_103/pdf); [GCK](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2020_SGCA_2/pdf); [Jumaat](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2025_SGCA_40/pdf).

[Inference] If s 107's accused-side legal burden applies to the identified exception facts and the evidence stays in equipoise on **that** issue, that burden has not been shown discharged; this does not itself settle whether the prosecution has proven **every offence element** beyond reasonable doubt. The court may need to consider defence material bearing on both questions. A technical `unresolved` status is neither a judicial finding of equipoise nor an acquittal. The precise s 84-specific treatment of overlapping evidence and any qualification of s 107 must be approved by a Singapore-qualified reviewer before any legal-status mapping.

## Medical and procedural boundaries

The statutory expression tests incapacity **at the act** and its causal link to unsoundness, not a diagnostic code. *Han John Han* [5]–[6] and *Tan Chor Jin* [20] caution that courts determine the legal question using relevant evidence; *Chia Moh Heng* [6] and *Han* [6] differ about categorical medical-evidence necessity, so this packet does not state one. *Tan Chor Jin* [19]–[26] treats old intoxication provisions separately; *Roszaidi* [86]–[93] contrasts a different MDA impairment test with s 84. Diminished responsibility, automatism and intoxication are **not** alternate section 84 leaves. [Han](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2007_SGHC_8/pdf); [Tan Chor Jin](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2008_SGCA_32/pdf); [Roszaidi](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2022_SGCA_75/pdf).

CPC ss 246–256C in the locked capture cover fitness and unsoundness procedure, not a single substantive s 84 test. *Tan Kok Meng* [23]–[27] distinguishes a **court-entered** s 84 acquittal, an act finding and a custody order under its then CPC. *Abdul Ghufran* [48]–[51] says a fitness-to-plead notional term rests on hypothetical assumptions and contrasts the s 251 factual inquiry. Their distinct procedural statuses require a separate charge/proceeding/disposition record if Yuho ever represents them; a rule result cannot create any of these. [Tan Kok Meng](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2021_SGCA_55/pdf); [Abdul Ghufran](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/gd/2025_SGHC_98/pdf).
