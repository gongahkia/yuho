# Criminal outcome boundary: provisional architecture decision

**Status:** proposal for maintainer and Singapore-qualified legal review, 13 September 2026. **Baseline:** `b7c6c91f910de83273a17ce5317d9ba0b5a19628`. This document changes no executable contract. The [source ledger](CRIMINAL-OUTCOME-SOURCE-LEDGER.md) records source category, pinpoint, access and verification limits. It is architecture research, not legal advice or an executable Singapore rule package.

## Executive recommendation and verdict

**Defer `OffenceAssessment-v1` as an executable eighth variant.** The seventh Haskell result already reports the requested root rule's `satisfied | not_satisfied | unresolved` status, every evaluated branch and its reason, and ordered traces. Repeating that value under an offence label gains no semantic capability and risks implying a finding about an offence that the input has not identified as a complete offence definition. Retain the existing **rule** terminology. The next enabling design boundary should be a versioned **reviewed model-scope and legal-source registration** that can distinguish an offence definition from a definition, exception, participation wrapper or other rule, and record what legal text and cross-Act material the model claims to cover. An externally supplied charge/count and proceeding record needs a separate boundary. Neither should be implemented from this report alone.

An eventual charge/disposition layer must be separate from the rule kernel and based on identified procedural records. A charge may be answered by a plea, amended, withdrawn, decided, appealed or retried without the kernel deriving any of those events. A selected statutory penalty declaration is only a candidate under Yuho's technical branch rules. Its term tree describes encoded punishment forms; neither is an imposed sentence. The Singapore sources in the ledger support these *category distinctions*, not Yuho's legal fidelity [L1–L10].

## Repository capability audit

The accepted [architecture](YUHO-REWRITE-ARCHITECTURE-PROPOSAL.md), [migration contract](MIGRATION-CONTRACT.md), [repository audit](REPOSITORY-AUDIT.md) and seven fragment specifications distinguish technical results from legal outcomes. The production [request](../../rewrite/haskell/schema/request.schema.json) and [result](../../rewrite/haskell/schema/result.schema.json) schemas have exactly seven closed fragment variants. The seventh request selects `root_rule` from a closed registry and supplies leaf statuses, sources, policy, exception targets, penalties, terms and presumptions. It has no charge, count, accused, actor, proceeding, adjudicator, plea, court order, appeal or sentence object. The Haskell [core types](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/Core/Types.hs), [proof result types](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/SuppliedProofStatus/Types.hs) and [presumption types](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/Presumption/Types.hs) likewise contain no first-class offence-definition or procedural identity.

The seventh [run path](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/Presumption/Run.hs) validates and resolves the complete presumption registry, computes effective leaves, then calls the existing three-valued rule evaluator and penalty selector. [Proof evaluation](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/SuppliedProofStatus/Evaluate.hs) aggregates final branch values: any S gives root S; otherwise any U gives root U; otherwise root N. It retains branch IDs, paths, reasons, requirement traces and exception observations. Definition-only rules have no executable branch and report `definition_only`, not vacuous S. The [seventh encoder](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/Presumption/Encode.hs) emits root `status`, ordered `rules`, `proof_observations`, `presumption_derivations`, selected declarations, selection traces and warnings. It keeps supplied status, direct projection and effective satisfaction distinct. `satisfied` is a technical result over the **modeled rule**, not a finding by a tribunal.

The [penalty selector](../../rewrite/haskell/src/Yuho/SuppliedProofStatus/Select.hs) acts directly on final-S **root branches**, reads an already evaluated leaf trace for each guard, preserves inherited supports and skips N, U and exception-defeated branches. It does not wait for a separate offence or charge outcome. [PenaltyTerms-v1](PENALTY-TERMS-V1.md) validates every term tree but attaches terms only to selected declaration IDs; it does not choose an alternative or impose punishment. [RegisteredPresumptionDerivations-v1](REGISTERED-PRESUMPTION-DERIVATIONS-V1.md) may change effective satisfaction but leaves the externally supplied status intact. Source hashes check request-byte consistency, not legal authority.

Legacy Python is migration evidence. [StatuteEvaluator._evaluate_ast](../../src/yuho/eval/statute_evaluator.py) computes sibling alternatives and penalties from satisfied branches **before** calling `_resolve_criminal_outcome`; it then lets that outcome resolver replace `overall_satisfied` and the selected penalties. `_resolve_criminal_outcome` maps ordinary true to `convicted`, ordinary false to `not_proved`, and an exception transition to `acquitted`, `reduced_to` or special disposition; it picks the first exceptional branch. That conflates structural rule evaluation with adjudication and permits an outcome transition to substitute a target's penalties. The [legacy outcome type](../../src/yuho/eval/outcomes.py) calls these values legal results, but has no court order identity. The [AST statute](../../src/yuho/ast/nodes.py) and [CanonicalStatute](../../src/yuho/ir/canonical.py) are provision/rule encodings with typed outcome transitions; they do not become charges merely because a title says “offence”. Corpus search found no `.yh` line declaring an `outcome`; grammar source contains outcome productions while generated parser artefacts lag, as the repository audit records. None of those partial surfaces is a normative outcome oracle.

Other legacy backends reinforce the naming hazard. [Z3 generation](../../src/yuho/verify/z3_solver.py) names an elements-and-exception Boolean a `conviction` atom; handwritten [Lean evaluation](../../mechanisation/Yuho/Eval.lean) uses `Statute.convicts`. The repository audit establishes that these are bounded abstractions without a production IR-to-proof bridge, not proof that a court disposition follows. Legacy facts may use truthiness, normalized-name lookup and false for absent data; [SuppliedProofStatus-v1](SUPPLIED-PROOF-STATUS-V1.md) deliberately rejects those inferences. The public README's broad assurance language is not a stronger contract.

## The ten distinct layers

| Layer | Question and current owner | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leaf proof status | What `proved`, `not_proved` or reasoned `unresolved` classification was externally supplied for this exact leaf ID? Sixth/seventh request. | No factual-truth or burden-discharge inference. |
| Effective satisfaction | What S/N/U follows after registered technical support routes? Seventh result. | Rebuttal removes one route, never proves negation. |
| Rule branch | Are inherited requirements technically S, N or U, or is the branch exception-defeated? Existing branch result/reason. | N and defeat are distinct; neither is an acquittal. |
| Offence definition assessment | How does a *reviewed, complete, versioned offence-definition model* evaluate? No such identified unit yet. | A rule title or root ID is insufficient. |
| Charge/count | Which versioned allegation, against which accused, on what alleged facts, is before which proceeding? No model. | A charge is not the statute text; alternatives and amendments need identity. |
| Plea | What formal answer was entered to a charge? No model. | A guilty plea is supplied procedure, not a generated proof-status map. |
| Disposition | What judgment or order was entered by an authorized adjudicator for that charge? No model. | Conviction, acquittal and discharge require distinct order types and provenance. |
| Penalty eligibility | Which encoded statutory declarations are selected from final-S root branches? Fourth through seventh results. | A selected declaration is not a sentence or court finding. |
| Sentence | What punishment did a court actually impose? No model. | Term trees describe encoded declarations, not judicial choice. |
| Appeal/finality | Was an order appealed, varied, quashed, retried or left operative? No model. | Store linked procedural events and their effective sequence; do not overwrite history. |

`not_proved` is supplied non-establishment, not innocence; `not_satisfied` is not an acquittal; `unresolved` is not a discharge; a rebutted route is not a court order. Nor does absence of a matching executable route establish a final judgment. The source ledger distinguishes directly viewed judicial material from indexed but presently inaccessible legislation [L1–L10].

## Legal-source findings and design implications

The official CPC index identifies separate charge content, amendment, plea, trial, withdrawal/discharge and appeal provisions [L1, L2, L4, L5]. The indexed current CPC text distinguishes a recorded guilty plea from a possible conviction (§227), a court's not-guilty/guilty finding and subsequent sentence (§230(1)(w)–(x)), and discharge with or without the effect of an acquittal (§232(2), subject to §147). It permits an altered or new charge before judgment (§128) and appellate changes to findings or sentence (§390). **Retrieval limit:** direct Singapore Statutes Online (SSO) page requests returned HTTP 403 on this run; the indexed extracts and exact version dates need point-in-time confirmation before legal modelling. Official Singapore Courts material independently separates charge, plea, conviction, acquittal and sentencing at an explanatory level [L8, L9].

The indexed Evidence Act §§103–104 and 107 distinguish a burden to prove asserted facts from a specified burden concerning exceptions [L6]; the indexed Penal Code §84 describes a general exception [L7]. These are **not** a general algorithm for allocating burdens in Yuho. In the directly displayed official *Public Prosecutor v GCK* judgment, [2020] SGCA 2, [1]–[3], the court identifies proof beyond reasonable doubt as an evidential standard, describes one charge and records a trial conviction followed by an appellate acquittal [L10]. The court, not a Boolean rule, assesses evidence. The official *Jumaat* judgment endpoint was serving an eLitigation maintenance notice; the Singapore Courts-hosted SMU **case brief**, not the judgment, was accessible [L11, L12]. No proposition from its substantive presumption analysis is encoded here or treated as independently verified judicial text. All Singapore-specific mapping, including statutory general exceptions, requires qualified review.

## Alternatives and decision

| Alternative | Value and testability | Costs, risks and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. `OffenceAssessment-v1` or more careful `OffenceDefinitionEvaluation-v1` | A distinct result could eventually name a reviewed offence-definition ID, legal source/version, scope and all supporting branches. It could be tested against source-reviewed examples. | **Defer.** With today's input it is only root status renamed. No completeness, offence role, accused, temporal/cross-Act applicability or reviewed doctrine gate exists. `OffenceDefinitionEvaluation` is the safer future name if those inputs arrive; `OffenceAssessment` risks being read as a charge finding. |
| B. Supplied charge/disposition records | Real added information: count, subject, charge version, plea, order, issuing authority, date and appellate history. Jurisdiction-general records can have explicit jurisdiction-specific extensions. | **Design separately after provenance and procedure review.** A source hash/issuer label cannot authenticate a court order. Different event and order types must not be collapsed into one status. |
| C. Combined outcome fragment / `ChargeOutcome-v1` | Easy migration from Python `CriminalOutcome`; superficially convenient. | **Reject.** It would map rule S/N to conviction/acquittal without charge, adjudication, source authority or appeal state, repeat the Python conflation, and make formal proofs about the technical algebra appear to prove legal outcomes. |
| D. Keep rule results; add an enabling boundary | No new semantic alias or wire-compatibility burden. Reviewable model-scope/source registration could establish whether a root is an offence definition and what it covers; charge records can be supplied separately. | **Recommend.** It requires independent source-version, cross-Act, completeness and legal-review gates before any offence-definition result. Existing seventh bytes remain stable. |

All options can be jurisdiction-general only if they keep the kernel's rule algebra distinct from jurisdiction-specific legal effect. A copied A-status gives low Singapore usefulness; a reviewed offence package and procedural event record give higher usefulness but materially greater migration and validation work. A new variant also requires schema, fixtures, proof-neutral vectors and exact prior-byte regression checks; D incurs none now. Proof of existing S/N/U algebra would not prove offence completeness or adjudication.

## Concrete answers to the boundary questions

1. **Unit now:** one selected root **rule**, with executable branch occurrences; not a person, charge, count or verified offence definition. The schema supplies no first-class procedural identity.
2. **Alternative branches:** the existing S-if-any, else U-if-any, else N aggregation is justified as *technical rule aggregation only*. Zero executable branches stay definition-only/N. Multiple S branches are all reported as support; none is chosen as controlling.
3. **Defeat and uncertainty:** an exception-defeated branch is N with a distinct reason; a U branch contributes U only if no sibling is S. Every branch remains in the trace. Presentation order is deterministic, not legal priority.
4. **Conclusion:** one S branch establishes only that this model's selected root rule is technically S under supplied classifications, registered derivations and the validated graph. It establishes no evidence sufficiency, offence completeness, guilt or adjudicative result.
5. **Penalties:** existing selection directly uses final-S root branches. An intervening assessment would not change selected IDs, supports, overlap warnings or term trees; if it did, it would be a separate new semantic rule requiring review.
6. **Attempt, abetment, conspiracy, participation and alternatives:** defer to separately modeled offence/participation relations, charge versions and reviewed cross-Act packages. Do not infer them from a rule title or `is_infringed` edge. CPC charge/alternative provisions in the indexed statute warrant later procedural analysis [L1, L2].
7. **Guilty plea:** record it as a supplied procedural event linked to a particular charge version. It may lead to a court conviction under applicable procedure [L4], but must neither manufacture leaf `proved` statuses nor be silently evaluated as an offence branch.
8. **Disposition provenance:** require a stable proceeding/count/order ID, exact charge version, court and adjudicator identity, order type, decision date/time, document/version identity, trustworthy source authentication and review/audit chain. Existing source hashes and free-text labels do not meet this gate. The exact jurisdiction-general envelope and Singapore adapter need separate design and legal review.
9. **Discharge, withdrawal and appeal:** use distinct dated events and orders, linked to affected charge IDs and earlier orders. Model discharge's acquittal effect explicitly, not by treating all withdrawals or N results as acquittals. An appeal can vary or quash a judgment and sentence; finality needs procedural status plus effective order history, not a Boolean on the rule result [L2, L5].
10. **New variant now:** no. Its only computable `status` and branch support are already present in the seventh variant; another field would be a semantic alias with a misleading name. The capability gate for a later variant is an independently validated offence-definition unit, not prettier vocabulary.

## Migration, risks and gates

Treat Python's ordinary S→`convicted`, N→`not_proved`, exception→`acquitted`/`reduced_to`, first exceptional transition, and outcome-driven penalty substitution as **review-and-correct**, never parity targets. Preserve only the technical source-ordered branch/trace and selected-declaration observations on their declared common subset. Legacy `OutcomeNode`, `CanonicalOutcomeTransition`, Z3 `conviction` atoms, Lean `Statute.convicts`, corpus phrases such as “liable”, and s84/CPC examples are migration evidence or abstract models. Parser acceptance, backend agreement and source hashes do not establish legal correctness. Adapter-only compatibility may render old labels alongside a conspicuous “legacy technical projection” notice; it must not emit court orders.

Before a genuine `OffenceDefinitionEvaluation-v1` could be proposed, require: (i) a stable offence-definition ID and explicit rule role; (ii) source version/effective-date and cross-Act scope, with missing legal material represented as incomplete rather than N; (iii) reviewed mapping of elements, exceptions, participation and alternatives; (iv) a defined subject/context boundary; (v) all supporting branches and defeat/U traces; (vi) a counterexample suite where the same rule result has different charge/procedural outcomes; (vii) a new closed schema only if the computation adds a field not derivable from seventh output; (viii) proof-neutral vectors that assert technical correspondence only; and (ix) exact byte preservation for the seven existing variants. Reverse the defer decision only if these yield a genuinely new computable invariant. If the goal is only a UI summary, render the existing root and branch results without a new protocol variant.

For a supplied charge/disposition design, first choose an authority-verification mechanism and a reviewable court-record source, identity for each count and amendment, treatment of pleas and consent, order/event history, appeal and retrial semantics, and the jurisdiction adapter. A Singapore-qualified reviewer must assess the CPC/EA/PC versions and pinpoints, completeness of general/special exceptions and burden allocation, plea and discharge effects, alternative/substituted charges, appellate/finality rules, and any section 84/CPC slice. A maintainer must decide whether model-scope registration or externally supplied charge records is the next *design* task and what users may see before review. **Recommended answer:** model-scope/source registration first; charge/disposition records second; do not expose offence, conviction or acquittal labels from the rule kernel.

No new truth table or wire shape is proposed as normative input. The existing root table is `any S → S; else any U → U; else N`, with `definition_only` explicit and all branch supports retained. The proposal neither chooses a proof tool nor claims formal refinement, evidential sufficiency or legal correctness.
