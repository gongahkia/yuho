# PenaltyTerms-v1

**Status:** Implemented technical KernelInput/KernelResult v1 fragment, 12 September 2026. This specification extends [GuardedPenaltySelection-v1](GUARDED-PENALTY-SELECTION-V1.md). Canonical IR v1.2, its serialization and hashes are unchanged.

## Meaning and boundary

The fifth closed fragment attaches one source-ordered punishment-description tree to every penalty declaration. It answers what structured terms the encoded declaration asserts. It does **not** select a punishment child, calculate or recommend a sentence, decide mandatory treatment, merge ranges or declarations, compare severity, resolve outcomes, apply sentencing discretion or cross-Act restrictions, or establish legal correctness. A selected declaration, its available punishment forms and prescribed bounds, and a sentence imposed in an individual case are different objects. [Singapore Courts' charge guide](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/criminal/representing-yourself-mentions-court/reading-charges) distinguishes prescribed ranges from the punishment chosen for a case; the [Chief Justice's Sentencing Conference 2022 address](https://www.judiciary.gov.sg/news-and-resources/news/news-details/chief-justice-sundaresh-menon-keynote-address-delivered-at-the-sentencing-conference-2022) describes judicial sentencing discretion. Both official pages were accessed 12 September 2026 for terminology only and did not review this fragment.

The request inherits the entire typed-fact, acyclic-exception and guarded-selection contract. In its [fifth closed request variant](../../rewrite/haskell/schema/request.schema.json), **every** penalty declaration has exactly one required `term` in addition to its fourth-fragment fields. The term's `source_id` is inherited from that declaration; the wire node must not repeat it. Every term node has a globally unique qualified `term_id` beginning `term:` and a valid UTF-8 byte span contained by both its parent term (if any) and the declaration span. Nodes are validated across the complete registry, including unselected, false, defeated, definition-only, unreachable and dependency-target declarations, before any rule evaluation. A missing or invalid term rejects the whole request, without a partial judgment.

## Closed tree algebra

Each node has `kind`, `term_id`, `span` and exactly the fields of its kind:

| `kind` | Additional required fields | Meaning in the encoded source |
|---|---|---|
| `all_of` | `terms`: ordered array of at least two nodes | Co-listed or conjoined forms. |
| `exactly_one_of` | `terms`: ordered array of at least two nodes | Exclusive alternatives are encoded. |
| `one_or_more_of` | `terms`: ordered array of at least two nodes | One or several listed forms are encoded, including “X or Y or both”. |
| `term_imprisonment` | `unit`, `minimum`, `maximum` | Nominal imprisonment duration bounds. |
| `life_imprisonment` | none | Categorical life form, with no numeric proxy. |
| `fine` | `currency`, `minimum`, `maximum` | SGD amount bounds. |
| `caning` | `minimum`, `maximum` | Stroke-count bounds. |
| `death` | none | Categorical death form. |

Every combinator retains its authored nesting and child order. No flattening, sorting, distribution, deduplication or algebraic rewriting occurs. Distinct term IDs remain distinct even for textually similar atoms. A term-tree root counts as depth 1; depth **16** is accepted and depth 17 rejects. All nodes count toward `policy.max_nodes` (1–1,024); the existing 4,096 expanded branch/declaration-occurrence cap is unchanged. The request and line cap is 1,048,576 bytes and the JSON container cap is 64. All fixed objects reject duplicate or unknown keys.

Each bounded atom has explicit `minimum` and `maximum` endpoint objects. `{"kind":"specified","value":5}` means the endpoint is encoded; `{"kind":"not_stated"}` means no endpoint was encoded; `{"kind":"unbounded"}` is an explicit open-end assertion. `not_stated` is neither zero nor infinity. `unbounded` is permitted **only** for a fine's maximum; it is never inferred from omitted text, a legacy zero or `fine_unlimited`. Minimums cannot be unbounded.

Term imprisonment uses exactly one of `days`, `weeks`, `months`, `years`, with specified JSON integer endpoints 1–1,000,000. Caning uses the same integer interval for strokes. The upper value is a technical safety limit, not a statutory maximum. If both endpoints are specified, minimum must not exceed maximum. No fractional or mixed duration, unit conversion, approximate days/seconds, life sentinel, or calendar arithmetic is part of this fragment. Life and death accept no bound, mandatory, discretionary or numerical fields.

Fine uses `currency:"SGD"` only. A specified amount is an exact JSON decimal **string** matching `(?:0|[1-9][0-9]{0,17})(?:\.[0-9]{1,2})?` and strictly greater than zero. The parser uses integer cents, never floating point; canonical output has exactly two fractional digits (`"1"` → `"1.00"`, `"1.2"` → `"1.20"`). Signs, exponent notation, commas, excess leading zeroes, more than 18 integer digits or two fractional digits, and zero reject. Two specified endpoints must be ordered. A fine with no amount encoded has `not_stated` at **both** endpoints, not an implicit unlimited maximum. There is no currency conversion or unknown-currency fallback.

For example, `one_or_more_of` with a five-year maximum `term_imprisonment` child and a `fine` child with both bounds `not_stated` records an authored “imprisonment or fine or both” structure. It authorizes no choice or sentence. A singleton `one_or_more_of`, a life node with `maximum`, a fine minimum of zero, an unbounded caning maximum and a child span outside its parent all reject.

## Validation, selection and result

The phase order is bounded duplicate-aware JSON decoding; existing source, ID, typed binding, graph, penalty declaration and guard validation; full-registry term ID, shape, span, bound and capability validation; pure root evaluation and unchanged penalty selection; then association of each selected ID with its validated term. The validated `penalty_id → Term` map is built before evaluation. There is no path from the term to facts, exceptions, branch truth, guard truth, selected IDs, supports or `KSEL001`. Dependency-target terms are validated but are not selected automatically for the root; exception-defeated branches still select nothing.

The [fifth result variant](../../rewrite/haskell/schema/result.schema.json) retains every fourth-fragment field and array-order rule. Each `selected_penalties` record gains exactly its declaration's canonical `term`; unselected terms are omitted from the judgment. Supporting paths, selection traces and overlap warnings remain unchanged. Overlapping selected declarations keep separate trees. A future inspection operation may show all validated terms, but this result does not. Sorted JSON mapping keys do not reorder source-ordered term children, selected declarations, supports or traces.

`KPROT001` is a protocol/version failure. `KDEC001/002` cover malformed JSON or primitive syntax and byte/depth limits under existing boundary conventions. `KINV009` covers duplicate/invalid term IDs, invalid bounds, illegal endpoint combinations, empty/singleton combinators and term containment; existing `KINV003` covers a span invalid against its source. `KCAP001` covers recognized unsupported atoms, non-SGD currency, sentencing metadata, cross-Act limits, compensation, forfeiture, disqualification, community/corrective/preventive detention, default imprisonment, suspended/concurrent/consecutive operation, person-specific eligibility and executable supplementary prose. Arbitrary extra fields use existing closed-object rejection. `KERR001` denotes an impossible missing selected term after validation. Malformed JSON may be rejected before the fragment is identifiable and therefore uses the generic pre-dispatch envelope. No rejection may be read as “no punishment terms selected.”

## Migration and assurance

The current Python `PenaltyNode` is migration evidence, not this wire schema. Intentional divergences include explicit `not_stated` instead of zero, no inferred `fine_unlimited`, no USD fallback, nominal duration units instead of approximate day conversion, exact decimal strings rather than floats or implicit cents, categorical life/death, rejection of contradictory bounds, explicit term IDs and spans, authored tree order, no reliance on lossy exporters, and no term influence on selection. The [migration probes](../../rewrite/haskell/test/term-fixtures/MIGRATION-PROBES.json) call out zero-bound ambiguity, s304A term payloads, “or both,” fine without amount, caning, life/death and mandatory minima. An adapter must report unmappable kinds, ambiguous or inferred endpoints, discarded prose, unsupported currencies/modes and any lossy mapping; it must not silently strip these. No corpus provision is promoted to reviewed doctrine here.

The [PT fixtures](../../rewrite/haskell/test/term-fixtures/CASES.json), [manifest](../../rewrite/haskell/test/term-fixtures/MANIFEST.json) and [proof-neutral vectors](../../rewrite/haskell/test/term-fixtures/PROOF-VECTORS.json) cover valid trees, canonical forms, selected ID/term associations and typed rejection classifications. A future reference relation can state that **successfully validated** equal fourth-fragment selection results plus equal validated penalty-ID/term maps yield equal fifth-fragment selected-term records, preserving authored order. This is a proposed relation, not a theorem, formal refinement, sentencing opinion or legal review. Proof-tool selection, proof statuses, authenticated provenance and stronger jurisdiction-specific doctrine remain separate decisions.
